What is a pen plotter?

A machine that holds a pen and draws on paper by moving it along programmed coordinates. Unlike a printer, it draws each line physically. The result is an ink drawing, not a reproduction.

How are the drawings made?

Each mobile suit is rebuilt as vector line work and compiled to a stroke program. An AxiDraw plotter executes the program with a real pen, one stroke at a time.

Are these prints?

No. Every copy is drawn individually by machine, in ink, on Bristol paper. Because ink flow varies with conditions, no two copies in an edition are identical.

What paper and ink are used?

Strathmore 300 Series Bristol, smooth, 11×14″, with Staedtler Triplus 0.3 mm fineliners. The ink is pigment-based, acid-free, and lightfast.

How long does a plot take?

It depends on the drawing. A typical 11×14″ edition takes twenty to thirty minutes of continuous drawing and lays down ten to thirty metres of ink. Each edition's plot time is computed from its stroke program and shown on the Live Plot.

What sizes are available?

Current editions are drawn on 11×14″ (279 × 356 mm) Bristol. Other sizes are possible through Commissions.

How are editions numbered?

Each edition is a run of 25. Copies are numbered in the order they are plotted and sold. An edition closes when the run is complete.

Do you take commissions?

Yes, a limited number. Custom subjects, sizes, and inks are quoted individually. See Commissions.

Do you ship internationally?

Yes, with tracked carriers. See Shipping for destinations, rates, and timing.

Can I return a piece?

Each copy is plotted to order, so returns for change of mind are not accepted. Copies damaged in transit are replaced. See Returns.
